## HCV Positive Mother.

```

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) recommends that infants born to mothers who are Hepatitis C positive should be tested for Hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection. The CDC advises that:  
Anti-HCV antibody testing should be performed no sooner than age 18 months because maternal antibodies can persist until that age.  
If earlier diagnosis is desired, HCV RNA testing can be performed at or after the infant’s first well-child visit at age 1-2 months. This test should be repeated at a subsequent visit to confirm the result.

Breastfeeding is also not contraindicated. The CDC and the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) state that breastfeeding is safe for mothers with Hepatitis C, as there is no significant association between breastfeeding and HCV transmission. However, the CDC recommends that mothers with cracked or bleeding nipples should temporarily refrain from breastfeeding and discard the milk from the affected breast until the nipples are healed to prevent potential blood exposure.

These recommendations ensure accurate diagnosis and appropriate follow-up for infants potentially exposed to HCV.
```
